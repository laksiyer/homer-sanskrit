# वकदाल्भ्यकथा तथा क्रूसेसस्य प्रार्थना

**Comparative study:** Iliad 1.8–100 · Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā 10.6 · Mahābhārata, Śalyaparvan (chapter 42 in one common numbering, chapter 40 in the Critical Edition).

> [!note]- Editorial status
> A close narrative and ritual parallel, **not** an established Indo-European lexical cognate, identification of Apollo with Rudra, or evidence of direct literary borrowing. The versions differ in their mechanisms and circumstances.

## Narrative comparison

| Homer's Iliad | Vedic and epic parallels |
| --- | --- |
| Chryses, a priest, approaches a king to secure his daughter's release (1.11–21). | Vaka Dālbhi approaches Dhṛtarāṣṭra Vaicitravīrya after a sattra, expecting appropriate gifts (KS 10.6). |
| Agamemnon rejects Chryses with harsh words (1.22–32). | Dhṛtarāṣṭra dismisses and insults Vaka (KS 10.6). |
| Chryses prays to Apollo (1.35–42). | Vaka performs an offering to **Agni Rudravat** using black rice (KS 10.6). |
| Apollo afflicts the Achaean camp (1.43–52). | Dhṛtarāṣṭra's wealth and domain are damaged; the ritual is explained through Agni's Rudra-form (KS 10.6). |
| Apollo's offense is diagnosed, and return plus expiation are demanded (1.92–100). | Dhṛtarāṣṭra must make amends to Vaka; Vaka performs an Agni Surabhimat rite for restoration (KS 10.6). |

## Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā 10.6 · Original Sanskrit excerpts

The following are **short extracts**, not the complete continuous Brāhmaṇa prose passage. Consult the linked edition for the full account and surrounding ritual prescriptions.

> नैमिष्या वै सत्रमासत ... तान्वको दाल्भिरब्रवीत् ...

The Naimiṣeyas conduct a sattra; Vaka Dālbhi proposes to seek support from Dhṛtarāṣṭra rather than divide the young cattle with the others.

> सोऽग्नये रुद्रवतेऽष्टाकपालं निरवपत् कृष्णानां व्रीहीणाम् ।

He makes an eight-cake offering of black rice to Agni Rudravat.

> अग्निर्वै रुद्रो रुद्रायैवैनमपिदधाति ।

The Brāhmaṇa interprets Agni as Rudra in the destructive ritual. The following explanation connects black grains with darkness and death. Restoration follows an offering to Agni Surabhimat using white rice.

**Complete Kāṭhaka text:** [Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā, Sthānaka 10, section 6](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/काठकसंहिता_(विस्वरः)/स्थानकम्_१०). Search the page for **“नैमिष्या वै सत्रम्”** or **“तान्वको दाल्भि”** to reach the episode.

## Mahābhārata · Śalyaparvan, Baka Dālbhi episode

This is an **expanded epic retelling**, not identical to the Saṃhitā narrative. In the commonly numbered Śalyaparvan 9.42, the sage's destructive ritual uses the flesh of dead cattle. His anger is directed at the king's kingdom, which subsequently declines; reconciliation follows. Unlike the Kāṭhaka version, the epic's ritual details foreground the cattle flesh rather than the Agni Rudravat black-rice iṣṭi.

> स तैरेव जुहावाग्नौ राष्ट्रं मांसैर्महातपाः ॥

With those pieces of flesh the ascetic offered the kingdom into the fire (chapter 42, verse 13 in the linked presentation; the corresponding passage is numbered differently in the Critical Edition).

> ततो विप्रादिकाः प्राहुः पशुविप्रकृतस्त्वया ।
> मांसैरभिजुहोतीदं तव राष्ट्रं मुनिर्बकः ॥

The counsellors explain that the king's offence against the priest over the animals underlies the calamity (chapter 42, verse 20 in the linked presentation).

**Complete epic passage:** [Mahābhārata, Śalyaparvan 9.42, Sanskrit with line-by-line transliteration and English](https://eternalraga.com/en/scriptures/mahabharata/shalya-parva/42). The linked edition prints the entire chapter, including material following this episode. Chapter numbering varies across editions; identify the passage by Vaka Dālbhi rather than number alone.

## Editorial assessment

**Shared narrative pattern:** insult to a religious specialist → effective appeal/ritual retaliation → harm to a ruler's people or realm → diagnosis → reconciliation/expiation.

**Differences:** Apollo is personally invoked and shoots plague-bearing arrows; Vaka acts through an offering with Rudra-associated Agni in the Kāṭhaka account. The epic recension describes a different ritual medium. The parallel is valuable, but does not demonstrate a single inherited myth, direct dependence, or identity of the Greek and Vedic deities.
