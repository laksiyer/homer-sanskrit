# Iliad 1.22–42 | Agamemnon's refusal and the priest's prayer

> [!warning] Working edition
> Greek and Sanskrit are aligned to Homer 1.22–42. Roman Greek is a simplified reading transliteration, not a reconstruction of ancient pronunciation. Sanskrit prose is provisional. English below is an independent close reading aid, not a quotation from Murray.

## Sanskrit, Devanāgarī

तदा अन्ये सर्वे अखैयाः याजकस्य सत्कारं कर्तुं शोभनं निष्क्रयधनं च प्रतिग्रहीतुं सम्मतिं प्रकटितवन्तः। किन्तु अत्रेयपुत्रस्य अगमेम्नोनस्य मनसि तत् न रोचते स्म। स क्रूसेसं परुषवचनैर्निरसृजत्, दृढां च आज्ञाम् अदात्।

“हे वृद्ध, त्वाम् अहम् एतासां अन्तःशून्यानां नौकानां समीपे मा पुनः पश्येयम्, नात्र चिरं तिष्ठन्तं न वा पश्चात् पुनरागच्छन्तम्। देवस्य दण्डः पवित्रमालाश्च त्वां न रक्षेयुः। तां कन्याम् अहं न मोक्ष्यामि। मम गृहे अर्गोस्-नगरे स्वपितृभूमेर्दूरे तस्याः जरापि आगमिष्यति। सा तत्र तन्तुवायकार्यं करिष्यति, मम शय्यां च उपगमिष्यति। अतः गच्छ, मां मा प्रकोपय। एवं निरुपद्रवः स्वगृहं प्रतिगमिष्यसि।”

एवम् उक्तवति तस्मिन् स वृद्धो भीतः तस्य वचनम् अन्वतिष्ठत्। स निःशब्दं बहुघोषस्य समुद्रस्य तीरेण जगाम। ततः दूरं गत्वा स वृद्धः लेतोः सुकेश्याः पुत्रम् अपोल्लोनं प्रभुं बहु प्रार्थयामास।

“हे रजतधन्वन्, मम वचनं शृणु। त्वं क्रूसे-प्रदेशं रक्षसि, अतिपवित्रां किल्लां च, तेनेदोस्-द्वीपं च बलेन शाससि। हे आखुपते, यदि कदाचित् तुभ्यं प्रीतिकरं देवायतनं मया आच्छादितम्, यदि वा वृषभाणाम् अजानां च मेदस्वीनि ऊरुमांसानि तुभ्यं अग्नौ हुतानि, तर्हि मे इमाम् अभिलाषां पूरय। तव बाणैः दानाओइजनाः मम अश्रूणां प्रतिशोधं दद्युः।”

## Comparative notes

**1.26, 1.33, 1.35: γέρων / जरन्त्.** Greek *gérōn* 'old man', with stem *geront-*, is cognate with Vedic *járant-* 'aged'. Our prose uses **वृद्ध** for its natural idiom, but the inherited form is recorded in [[Comparative Lexicon]].

**1.30: πατρίς / पितृ.** Greek *patrís* 'fatherland' is derived from the inherited father-word and the Sanskrit translation **पितृभूमिः** preserves that transparent association. Compare Greek πατήρ ~ Sanskrit पितृ (established); do not claim that the entire derivatives are identical words.

**1.26: νῆες / नौ-.** Homeric ships and Sanskrit **नौ-** belong to the same inherited word family. The compound 'hollow ships' is here explicated in prose.

**1.37: ἀργυρότοξος / रजतधन्वन्.** A close semantic epithet “silver-bowed”, not an asserted lexical cognate. It is distinct from **दूरेहेतिः**, the preferred Vedic expression for far-shooting Apollo in 1.14 and 1.21 (TS 3.4.7.2; compare दूरेवधः in TS 4.5.8).

**Vedic deity comparison (1.35–42): Apollo and Rudra.** Apollo is invoked as the silver-bowed archer capable of sending his arrows against the Achaeans. Vedic **Rudra (रुद्र)** is an instructive *functional and poetic comparison*: he too is addressed as a bow-bearing god whose weapons can harm and from whom protection and healing are sought (Ṛgveda 2.33; TS 4.5.8). These are **not** established cognate divine names. We retain **अपोल्लोनः** and do not translate it as रुद्रः. See [Ṛgveda 2.33](https://rigveda-online.github.io/2/33.html), [TS 4.5.8](https://yajurveda.sanatana.in/ts/anuvaka/4/5/8/) and [Sāyaṇa on दूरेहेतिः, TS 3.4.7](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/saMhitA/sAyaNaH/3/4/07/).

**1.39: Σμινθεῦ (*Smintheu*) / आखुपते.** We use **आखुपतिः** (vocative **आखुपते**) as our preferred *interpretive* Sanskrit rendering of Apollo's regional cult epithet. Ancient explanations associate the epithet with σμίνθος, “mouse”; its etymology and cult history remain debated. This is **not an established Indo-European cognate**. Preserve the Greek name in the source text and romanization. [Homer, Iliad 1.39 (Venetus A)](https://www.homermultitext.org/docs/venetus-a/12v/).

**Rudra and the mouse, TS 1.8.6.** **आखुस्ते रुद्र पशुस्तं जुषस्व।** Padaccheda: आखुः । ते । रुद्र । पशुः । तम् । जुषस्व । “The mouse is your animal, O Rudra; accept it.” This is a **ritual parallel**, not proof that Apollo and Rudra were identical. Interpretations of the animal and its ritual significance must be read in context. [TS 1.8.6 text and commentary](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/saMhitA/sarva-prastutiH/1/8_rAjasUyAdi/06_chAturmAsyeShu_sAkamedhaparva-tryambaka-haviH/).

**Agni as a mouse, Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 1.1.3.** **अग्निर्देवेभ्यो निलायत । आखू रूपं कृत्वा । स पृथिवीं प्राविशत् ।** Agni hides from the gods, takes the form of a mouse, and enters the earth; the narrative also explains **आखुकरीष** in the ritual collection of materials. This is a **separate Vedic mouse motif**, not a second Apollo–Rudra identification. [TB 1.1.3, Sanskrit text with commentary](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/brAhmaNam/sarva-prastutiH/1/1_agnyAdheyAdi/03_deva-yajanArtha-sambhArAH/).

**1.41: αἰγῶν / अजानाम्.** Both denote goats; the historical etymological relationship has competing analyses and is not classified as securely established in this edition.

**1.42: Δαναοί / दानाओइजनाः.** We retain this Greek ethnonym as a Sanskritized name. Do not replace it with Sanskrit दानवाः, which refers to a different mythological category and is not an established cognate.

## English reading aid (collapsible)

> [!note]- English translation (show when needed)
> All the other Achaeans approved respecting the priest and accepting the splendid ransom, but the proposal did not please Agamemnon, son of Atreus. He dismissed the priest harshly and commanded him: “Old man, do not let me find you at the hollow ships, lingering now or returning later. Your god's staff and sacred wreath may not protect you. I will not release the girl. She will grow old in my house at Argos, far from her native land, working at the loom and sharing my bed. Go, and do not provoke me, so that you may return unharmed.” The old man feared him and obeyed. He went silently along the shore of the loud-roaring sea and, once far away, prayed earnestly to Apollo, son of lovely-haired Leto: “Hear me, silver-bowed god who guards Chryse and holy Cilla and rules Tenedos by might. Smintheus, if I have ever roofed a temple pleasing to you, or burnt the rich thigh pieces of bulls and goats for you, fulfill this wish: let the Danaans repay my tears with your arrows.”
>
