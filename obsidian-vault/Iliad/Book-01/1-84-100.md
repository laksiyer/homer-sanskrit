# Iliad 1.84–100 | Achilles promises safety; Calchas reveals Apollo’s anger

> [!warning] Working scholarly draft
> The Greek is the base text. Roman Greek is an accent-free reading transliteration. Sanskrit prose and IAST should be reviewed before final publication. English is our own close reading aid, not a quotation from Murray.

## Sanskrit, Devanāgarī

तं शीघ्रपादः अखिल्लेउसः प्रत्युवाच। “निर्भयः सन् यत् दैववचनं जानासि तत् सर्वं कथय। दिवः प्रियस्य अपोल्लोनस्य शपथेन वदामि, यस्य प्रार्थनया त्वं दानाओइजनेभ्यः दैववचनानि प्रकाशयसि, मयि जीवति पृथिवीं पश्यति च सति, एतासां गर्भवतीनां नौकानां समीपे कोऽपि दानाओइजनः त्वयि हिंसार्थं हस्तं न प्रसारयिष्यति। अगमेम्नोनम् अपि यदि निर्दिशेः, तथापि न कश्चित् त्वां स्प्रक्ष्यति। स हि इदानीं स्वयम् अखैयानां श्रेष्ठतम इति घोषयति।”

ततः स निर्दोषो दैवज्ञः निर्भयो भूत्वा अवदत्। “न प्रार्थनाया अभावात्, न च शतपशुयागस्य अभावात् अपोल्लोनः अस्मासु कुपितः। किं तु याजकस्य अवमाननात्। अगमेम्नोनः तस्य दुहितरं न मुमोच, निष्क्रयधनं च न जग्राह। अत एव दूरेहेतिः अस्मभ्यं दुःखानि अदात्, पुनरपि दास्यति। स दानाओइजनेभ्यः एतां घोराम् आपदं न निवारयिष्यति, यावत् तां विशालाक्षीं कन्यां तस्य प्रियाय पित्रे मूल्यं विना निष्क्रयं विना च प्रत्यर्पयेम, क्रूसेनगरं च पवित्रं शतपशुयागं नयेम। ततः तं प्रसाद्य तस्य अनुग्रहं प्राप्नुयाम।”

## Sanskrit, IAST

taṃ śīghrapādaḥ akhilleusaḥ pratyuvāca. “nirbhayaḥ san yat daivavacanaṃ jānāsi tat sarvaṃ kathaya. divaḥ priyasya apollonasya śapathena vadāmi, yasya prārthanayā tvaṃ dānāoijanebhyaḥ daivavacanāni prakāśayasi, mayi jīvati pṛthivīṃ paśyati ca sati, etāsāṃ garbhavatīnāṃ naukānāṃ samīpe ko'pi dānāoijanaḥ tvayi hiṃsārthaṃ hastaṃ na prasārayiṣyati. agamemnonam api yadi nirdiśeḥ, tathāpi na kaścit tvāṃ sprakṣyati. sa hi idānīṃ svayam akhaiyānāṃ śreṣṭhatama iti ghoṣayati.”

tataḥ sa nirdoṣo daivajñaḥ nirbhayo bhūtvā avadat. “na prārthanāyā abhāvāt, na ca śatapaśuyāgasya abhāvāt apollonaḥ asmāsu kupitaḥ. kiṃ tu yājakasya avamānanāt. agamemnonaḥ tasya duhitaraṃ na mumoca, niṣkrayadhanaṃ ca na jagrāha. ata eva dūrehetiḥ asmabhyaṃ duḥkhāni adāt, punarapi dāsyati. sa dānāoijanebhyaḥ etāṃ ghorām āpadaṃ na nivārayiṣyati, yāvat tāṃ viśālākṣīṃ kanyāṃ tasya priyāya pitre mūlyaṃ vinā niṣkrayaṃ vinā ca pratyarpayema, krūsenagaraṃ ca pavitraṃ śatapaśuyāgaṃ nayema. tataḥ taṃ prasādya tasya anugrahaṃ prāpnuyāma.”

## Comparative notes

**1.84: πόδας ὠκὺς / शीघ्रपादः.** A recurring Homeric formula, translated semantically rather than as an alleged cognate.

**1.86: Διῒ φίλον / दिवः प्रियस्य.** Same established Zeus–Dyaus cognate as at line 1.63. The reference remains to Zeus.

**1.88: ζῶντος / जीवति.** These are a semantic match; Greek *zaō* and Sanskrit *jīv-* must **not** be treated here as an assured lexical cognate without etymological support.

**1.89: νηυσί / नौकानाम्.** Greek *naus* and Sanskrit *nau-* are established cognates. Here the Greek is dative plural, and the Sanskrit phrase is governed by समीपे.

**1.93–96: ἱερῆα, θύγατρα / याजकः, दुहितरम्.** Greek *thugatēr* and Sanskrit *duhitṛ* are established Indo-European kinship cognates. The priest title is a semantic rendering.

**1.96: ἑκηβόλος / दूरेहेतिः.** As previously agreed, retain the Vedic expression दूरेहेतिः for Apollo's far-shooting epithet. Compare TS 3.4.7.2 and Sāyaṇa's explanation, दूरेऽप्यवस्थितस्य प्रहारसाधनं हेतिर्यस्यासौ दूरेहेतिः। This is a poetic/semantic parallel rather than a verified cognate.

**1.98–100: ἀπριάτην ἀνάποινον.** Two emphatic Greek terms: without purchase and without ransom. Both should remain explicit. Calchas also requires the sacred hecatomb to be brought to Chryse before Apollo may be appeased.

**Vedic deity comparison.** Apollo and Rudra share important poetic imagery of divine missiles, disease, healing and the far-reaching weapon (e.g. TS 3.4.7.2; TS 4.5.8; Atharvaveda Śaunaka 11.2.12). Their names are not established cognates, nor are the characters interchangeable.

## English reading aid (collapsible)

> [!note]- English translation (show when needed)
> Swift-footed Achilles replied: Speak confidently whatever divine message you know. By Apollo, beloved of Zeus, whom you invoke when revealing divine counsels to the Danaans, no Danaan will lay a heavy hand upon you by the hollow ships while I am alive and see the earth, not even if you name Agamemnon, who now boasts that he is greatest among the Achaeans.
> 
> The blameless seer then took courage: Apollo does not blame our prayers or hecatombs. He is angry because Agamemnon dishonored his priest, did not release his daughter, and did not accept the ransom. For that reason the far-shooter has sent, and will continue to send, suffering. He will not turn the terrible plague away from the Danaans until we return the bright-eyed girl to her dear father, without price or ransom, and bring a sacred hecatomb to Chryse. Then perhaps we may appease him.
