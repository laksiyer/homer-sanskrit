# Iliad 1.1–7 | The wrath of Achilles

> [!warning] Working edition
> Greek text checked against Perseus. Roman Greek is a simplified reading transliteration without accents or breathing marks. Sanskrit is an editable prose draft, not a vetted critical edition.

## Greek, reading transliteration

```text
1. mēnin aeide thea pēlēiadeō akhilēos
2. oulomenēn, ē muri᾽ akhaiois alge᾽ ethēke,
3. pollas d᾽ iphthimous psukhas aidi proiapsen
4. ērōōn, autous de elōria teukhe kunessin
5. oiōnoisi te pasi, dios d᾽ eteleieto boulē,
6. ex ou dē ta prōta diastētēn erisante
7. atreidēs te anax andrōn kai dios akhilleus.
```

## Greek, original polytonic

```text
1. μῆνιν ἄειδε θεὰ Πηληϊάδεω Ἀχιλῆος

3. πολλὰς δ᾽ ἰφθίμους ψυχὰς Ἄϊδι προΐαψεν

6. ἐξ οὗ δὴ τὰ πρῶτα διαστήτην ἐρίσαντε

```

## Sanskrit, Devanāgarī

हे देवि, पेलियसपुत्रस्य अखिल्लेउसस्य तं विनाशकरं मन्युं गाय।
स हि अखैयानां बहूनि दुःखानि जनयामास। बहूनां पराक्रमिणां वीराणां प्राणान् हैदेसं प्राहिणोत्। तेषां देहान् श्वभ्यः सर्वेभ्यश्च पक्षिभ्यः भक्ष्यत्वेन अकल्पयत्। एवं दिवः सङ्कल्पः सिद्धिमगच्छत्।
यतः प्रथमम् अत्रेयपुत्रो नराणामधिपतिरगमेम्नोनः दिव्यश्च अखिल्लेउसः विवादेन परस्परं पृथगभूताम्।

## English reading aid (collapsible)

> [!note]- English translation (show when needed)
> Sing, goddess, of the ruinous wrath of Achilles, son of Peleus, which brought countless sufferings upon the Achaeans and sent many mighty heroes’ souls to Hades, while making their bodies prey for dogs and birds. Thus the will of Zeus was fulfilled, from the time when the son of Atreus, lord of men, and godlike Achilles first parted in strife.
>
> Independent close translation prepared for this project. Reference editions: Samuel Butler (1898) and A. T. Murray (1924–25), available via Perseus.
