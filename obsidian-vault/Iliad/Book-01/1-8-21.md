### Vedic poetic parallel: दूरेहेतिः, Sāyaṇa on TS 3.4.7.2

At *Iliad* 1.14 and 1.21, Homer's **ἑκηβόλος** (*hekēbolos*, “far-shooting”) is rendered by the **attested Vedic expression दूरेहेतिः** (*dūrehetiḥ*, “one whose missile can strike at a distance”). In Sanskrit syntax the passage uses **दूरेहेतेः** (genitive, 1.14) and **दूरेहेतिम्** (accusative, 1.21).

**Vedic source:** Taittirīya Saṃhitā **3.4.7.2**:

> दू॒रेहे॑तिरमृड॒यो मृ॒त्युर्ग॑न्ध॒र्वः ।

**Sāyaṇa's commentary:**

> दूरेऽप्यवस्थितस्य प्रहारसाधनं हेतिर्यस्यासौ दूरेहेतिः।

**English:** “He whose *heti* (weapon or instrument of striking) can strike someone even when situated far away is *dūrehetiḥ*.” This is an explanatory rendering of Sāyaṇa's gloss.

**Contextual caution:** In this Vedic passage the designation occurs in a description of **Mṛtyu as a Gandharva**, not as an epithet addressed to Rudra. The parallel is nevertheless exceptionally useful for Apollo's far-shooting epithet because it preserves the notion of a weapon with distant reach.

**Compare:** Śrī Rudram, TS **4.5.8**, **नमो अग्रेवधाय च दूरेवधाय च** (“homage to the striker in front and the striker from afar”). **दूरेवधः** is a second Vedic poetic parallel, now *not* our preferred Homeric rendering.

**Classification:** semantic/poetic parallel, **not an established Greek–Sanskrit etymological cognate**. This translation does not equate Apollo with Rudra or with Mṛtyu.

## English reading aid (collapsible)

> [!note]- English translation (show when needed)
> Which god drove the two to quarrel and fight? The son of Leto and Zeus. Angered with the king, he stirred up a deadly plague throughout the army, and the people perished, because the son of Atreus had dishonored Chryses, the priest. Chryses had come to the Achaeans’ swift ships to ransom his daughter, bringing a great ransom and carrying on a golden staff the sacred wreaths of far-shooting Apollo. He pleaded with all the Achaeans, especially the two sons of Atreus, commanders of the host: ‘Sons of Atreus, and you other well-greaved Achaeans, may the gods who dwell on Olympus grant you to sack Priam’s city and return safely home. But release my dear daughter to me and accept the ransom, in reverence for Apollo the far-shooter, son of Zeus.’
>
