# Iliad 1.61–83 | Achilles proposes consultation; Calchas seeks protection

> [!warning] Working scholarly draft

## Sanskrit, Devanāgarī

यदि युद्धश्च रोगश्च युगपद् अखैयान् अभिभवतः, तर्हि अस्माकं विनाशो भविष्यति। अतः कञ्चिद् दैवज्ञं वा याजकं वा स्वप्नव्याख्यातारं वा पृच्छामः। स्वप्नोऽपि हि दिवः सकाशाद् आगच्छति। स अस्मभ्यं कथयेत्, किमर्थं फोइबः अपोल्लोनः एतावान् क्रुद्धः। किं स प्रार्थनायाः शतपशुयागस्य वा अभावं निन्दति? अथवा निर्दोषाणां मेषाणाम् अजाणां च मेदसा तुष्टः सन् अस्माकं विनाशं निवारयितुम् इच्छति?

इत्युक्त्वा अखिल्लेउसः उपाविशत्। ततः पक्षिशकुनज्ञेषु श्रेष्ठः थेस्तोरपुत्रः काल्खासः उत्थितः। स भूतं वर्तमानं भविष्यच्च जानाति स्म। फोइबेन अपोल्लोनेन दत्तया दैवज्ञविद्यया स अखैयानां नौः इलियोननगरं प्रति नीतवान् आसीत्। स तेषां हितं चिन्तयन् एवम् अवदत्।

“हे दिवः प्रिय अखिल्लेउस्, त्वं मां दूरेहेतेः प्रभोरपोल्लोनस्य मन्युं प्रकाशयितुम् आज्ञापयसि। अहं कथयिष्यामि। त्वं तु मम वचनं शृणु, शपथं च कुरु यत् वचसा हस्ताभ्यां च मां रक्षिष्यसि। अहं हि तादृशं नरं क्रोधयिष्यामीति मन्ये, यः सर्वेषाम् अर्गेयाणाम् अधिपतिः, यस्य आज्ञाम् अखैयाः अनुतिष्ठन्ति। स्वस्माद् दुर्बले नरे कुपितो राजा बलवत्तरः। स यदि तस्मिन्नेवाहनि क्रोधं निगृह्णीयात्, तथापि हृदये वैरं धारयति, यावत् तत् साधयति। अतः त्वं विचारय, मां रक्षिष्यसि वा।”

## Comparative notes

**1.63: ὄναρ ἐκ Διός / स्वप्नोऽपि दिवः सकाशात्.** Greek *Diós* and Vedic *Diváḥ* are inflected representatives of the established Zeus–Dyaus name cognacy. Homer refers to Zeus specifically; this is not merely a statement about the sky.

**1.62–63: μάντις, ἱερεύς, ὀνειροπόλος / दैवज्ञः, याजकः, स्वप्नव्याख्याता.** These are functional translations, **not** proposed lexical cognates. The distinct Greek offices are preserved.

**1.65–67: ἑκατόμβη / शतपशुयागः.** The Greek *hekatombē* literally evokes a hundred oxen, but is a conventional term for a major animal sacrifice; the number of victims was not necessarily one hundred. Our Sanskrit is explanatory, not a claim of identical Vedic ritual procedure.

**1.69–72: Κάλχας / काल्खासः.** Calchas is a Greek seer particularly skilled in interpreting birds. The Sanskrit दैवज्ञ and पक्षिशकुनज्ञ are semantic renderings; they do not identify his practice with a specific Vedic ritual office.

**1.70: τά τ’ ἐόντα τά τ’ ἐσσόμενα πρό τ’ ἐόντα.** The threefold temporal formula is rendered भूतं वर्तमानं भविष्यच्च. Greek *eon-* and Sanskrit *sat-/as-* both belong to the inherited Indo-European ‘be’ verb family, although these are different inflectional forms.

**1.75: μῆνιν / मन्युम्.** The running translation follows our editorial choice **मन्यु**. Vedic **मेनि** is a disputed comparative possibility and must not be listed as an established cognate. Greek **ἑκατηβελέταο** denotes Apollo's far-reaching arrow; **दूरेहेतिः** is an interpretive Vedic poetic parallel, cf. TS 3.4.7.2 and Sāyaṇa, not a lexical cognate.

**1.80–83: χόλος, κότος / क्रोधः, वैरम्.** Calchas distinguishes momentary anger from resentment that persists and seeks fulfillment. Sanskrit क्रोध and वैर retain that contrast; no lexical cognates are claimed.

## English reading aid (collapsible)

> [!note]- English translation (show when needed)
> If war and plague together overcome the Achaeans, we may have to turn back if we are to escape death. Let us consult a seer, a priest, or an interpreter of dreams, for dreams too come from Zeus. Let someone tell us why Phoebus Apollo is so angry: does he fault a prayer or a hecatomb, or will the smoke of perfect lambs and goats persuade him to avert disaster from us?
> 
> After speaking, Achilles sat down. Calchas, son of Thestor and foremost of bird-diviners, stood up. He knew what was, what would be, and what had been, and by Apollo's gift of prophecy had guided the Achaean ships to Ilium. Wishing them well, he addressed Achilles: You ask me to explain the anger of far-shooting Apollo. I will speak, but first promise to protect me in word and deed. I fear angering a man who rules all the Argives and whom the Achaeans obey. A king is stronger than a lesser man: he may swallow his anger today yet cherish a grudge until he carries it out. Consider whether you will protect me.

> Independent close translation prepared for this project; A. T. Murray (1924–25) is the reference edition, not reproduced verbatim.
