# Editorial principles · अनुवादरीतिः

1. Preserve the Greek original as source of truth, with precise book and line identifiers.
2. Prefer firmly established Greek–Sanskrit Indo-European cognates when their actual context and meaning permit.
3. Distinguish established cognates, disputed etymologies, semantic translations, and adapted names.
4. Retain a Greek god’s narrative identity even when rendering an established cognate (e.g. Zeus/Dyaus).
5. Use **मन्यु** for μῆνις in the running text; discuss Vedic **मेनि** only as a possible, unproven comparison.
6. Sanskrit prose is canonical for now. Compose anuṣṭubh as a secondary optional rendering after textual review.
7. Retain Greek script and record reading Romanization separately; never mistake transliteration for translation.
8. No automatic equivalence of Apollo with Indian deities or of θεός with देव as a claimed cognate.
9. Every borrowed Sanskrit expression needs an attribution and license review.
10. Track revision state and philological sources; do not publish provisional Sanskrit as critically verified.
