# Greek–Sanskrit comparative lexicon

Statuses are deliberately conservative.

## μῆνις (mēnis) → मन्यु

**Status:** semantic; possible etymologies disputed  
**Lines:** 1.1

Manyu is our chosen literary translation. Vedic मेनि is a comparative lead only, not an established cognate. Consult the actual Vedic occurrence and an etymological dictionary before asserting a connection.

## Διός (Dios) → दिवः

**Status:** established cognate  
**Lines:** 1.5, 1.9, 1.21

The Zeus/Dyaus name belongs to an inherited Indo-European sky-god word family. Keep Zeus as the character; do not reduce him to an anonymous sky.

## θύγατρα (thugatra) → दुहितरम्

**Status:** established cognate  
**Lines:** 1.13

Greek thugatēr and Sanskrit duhitṛ are inherited kinship terms; the Greek accusative is matched with Sanskrit accusative.

## νῆας (nēas) → नौः

**Status:** established cognate  
**Lines:** 1.12

Greek naus and Sanskrit nau- belong to the same inherited ship/boat word family.

## θεοί (theoi) → देवाः

**Status:** semantic equivalent only  
**Lines:** 1.18

Do not claim that Greek theos and Sanskrit deva are established cognates.

## Ἀπόλλων (Apollōn) → अपोल्लोनः

**Status:** Greek name retained  
**Lines:** 1.14, 1.21

No secure Sanskrit divine-name cognate is being asserted; similarity of function is not etymological identity.

## ἑκηβόλος (hekēbolos) → दूरेहेतिः

**Status:** Vedic semantic/poetic parallel, **not established lexical cognate**  
**Lines:** Iliad 1.14, 1.21

Preferred Sanskrit **दूरेहेतिः** (“one whose weapon reaches from afar”), attested TS **3.4.7.2** in a passage describing **Mṛtyu as a Gandharva**. Sāyaṇa glosses:

> दूरेऽप्यवस्थितस्य प्रहारसाधनं हेतिर्यस्यासौ दूरेहेतिः।

“He whose weapon is a means of striking someone even far away.” Sanskrit inflections in our passage: **दूरेहेतेः** (genitive) and **दूरेहेतिम्** (accusative).

**Compare**, without substituting in the running translation: **दूरेवधः**, TS **4.5.8**, Śrī Rudram: **नमो अग्रेवधाय च दूरेवधाय च**. This parallel highlights striking from afar, while **दूरेहेतिः** highlights the long reach of the missile itself. Neither expression is proven cognate to Greek ἑκηβόλος, nor does the comparison identify Apollo with a Vedic deity.

Source: [Sāyaṇa commentary, TS 3.4.7](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/saMhitA/sAyaNaH/3/4/07/)

## ἄποινα (apoina) → निष्क्रयधनम्

**Status:** semantic equivalent only  
**Lines:** 1.13, 1.20

Ransom or compensation offered to free Chryses’ daughter; not a cognate claim.
## γέρων (gerōn) → जरन् / जरन्त्

**Status:** established Indo-European cognate (Greek γέρων ~ Vedic járant-)  
**Lines:** 1.26, 1.33, 1.35

Greek γέρων, stem γεροντ-, 'aged man', is related to Vedic जरन्त् (*járant-*, 'old, aged') via the inherited aging root, including comparable participial morphology. In the running prose **वृद्ध** or **जरन्** may be used according to idiom. Evidence: IE-CoR Cognate Set 2825 (https://iecor.clld.org/cognatesets/2825), citing Beekes and Mayrhofer.

## πατρίς (patris) → पितृ / पितृभूमिः

**Status:** established cognate of the underlying kinship base, not necessarily a word-for-word cognate of the whole derivative  
**Lines:** 1.30

Greek πατρίς 'fatherland' is built on the Greek word for father, πατήρ, whose Sanskrit cognate is पितृ. Sanskrit **पितृभूमिः** faithfully brings out the inherited root but is a Sanskrit formation, not an asserted one-to-one cognate of the Greek derived word.

## αἰγῶν (aigōn) → अज / अजानाम्

**Status:** proposed/contested historical relationship; semantic equivalent in the running translation  
**Lines:** 1.41

Greek αἴξ (gen. αἰγός) and Sanskrit अज are both 'goat'; their historical relationship is debated in modern reconstructions, particularly the differences between Greek *aig-* and Indo-Iranian *ag-*. We use **अजानाम्** for fidelity while marking the etymology as uncertain.

## νυκτί (nykti) → नक्त् / नक्तमिव

**Status:** established Indo-European cognate, night-word family  
**Lines:** 1.47

Greek *nykt-* and Vedic **नक्त्** preserve inherited night vocabulary. The Greek dative and Sanskrit adverb are not identical inflectional forms. The comparison is linguistic, not a mythological identification.

**Source:** [Greek passage 1.47](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.47)

## κύνας (kunas) → शुनः

**Status:** established Indo-European cognate  
**Lines:** 1.50

Greek **κύων**, stem *kyn-*, and Sanskrit **श्वन्** (with accusative plural **शुनः** used in the prose) inherit the Indo-European word for dog.

**Source:** [Greek passage 1.50](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.50)

## ἐννῆμαρ (ennēmar) → नवाहानि

**Status:** established cognate of numeral 'nine'; compound is a new equivalent  
**Lines:** 1.53

Greek **ἐννέα** and Sanskrit **नव** are inherited numeral cognates. Here both render a nine-day duration.

**Source:** [Greek passage 1.53](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.53)

## δεκάτῃ (dekatēi) → दशमेऽहनि

**Status:** established cognate of numeral 'ten'  
**Lines:** 1.54

Greek **δέκα** and Sanskrit **दश** preserve the inherited numeral for ten; the Greek and Sanskrit ordinal constructions are separately inflected.

**Source:** [Greek passage 1.54](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.54)

## Ἥρη (Hērē) → हेरा

**Status:** Greek name retained; Śacī/Indrāṇī is a limited functional comparison, NOT a cognate
**Lines:** 1.55

**Vedic comparison:** **शची / इन्द्राणी (Śacī / Indrāṇī)** as Indra's consort is a useful *role-based analogy* to Hera as Zeus's consort. There is no demonstrated common inherited name or general identity. Hera's decision to move Achilles to convene the assembly must be read in its Greek narrative context. Retain **हेरा** in the running Sanskrit translation. Homer calls her **λευκώλενος**, 'white-armed', rendered **श्वेतबाहुः**.

Source: [Homer, Iliad 1.55 (Greek)](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.55)


## Φοῖβος Ἀπόλλων (Phoibos Apollōn) → फोइबः अपोल्लोनः

**Status:** Greek name retained; Rudra comparison is FUNCTIONAL / POETIC ONLY, NOT a cognate
**Lines:** 1.36, 1.37, 1.42, 1.43, 1.48, 1.49, 1.50, 1.51, 1.52, 1.53

**Vedic comparison:** **रुद्र (Rudra)**. Like the far-shooting Apollo, Vedic Rudra is a bow-bearing god whose weapons can afflict, and whose favor may preserve or heal. **Ṛgveda 2.33** invokes Rudra's bow and healing power; **TS 4.5.8** describes **दूरेवधः**. The closer epithet we chose, **दूरेहेतिः**, is attested in **TS 3.4.7.2**, where it refers to **Mṛtyu as a Gandharva**, not directly to Rudra; Sāyaṇa explains: **दूरेऽप्यवस्थितस्य प्रहारसाधनं हेतिर्यस्यासौ दूरेहेतिः।** None of this makes Apollo and Rudra identical or their names cognate. The narrative retains **अपोल्लोनः**.

Sources: [Ṛgveda 2.33](https://rigveda-online.github.io/2/33.html), [Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5.8](https://yajurveda.sanatana.in/ts/anuvaka/4/5/8/), [Sāyaṇa on TS 3.4.7](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/saMhitA/sAyaNaH/3/4/07/)

## Λητώ (Lētō) → लेतोः

**Status:** Greek name retained; no secure Vedic deity cognate
**Lines:** 1.36

**Vedic comparison:** **अदिति (Aditi)** may be compared *only in the broad maternal role* as a mother of gods, not as an etymological or mythological equivalent of Leto. Their mythic genealogies differ. Preserve **लेतोः** in the running translation.

Source: [Homer, Iliad 1.36](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.36)

## οὐρῆας (ourēas) → अश्वतरान्

**Status:** semantic equivalent, no cognate claimed
**Lines:** 1.50

The first animals struck by Apollo's arrows are mules, not donkeys. Sanskrit **अश्वतरान्** reflects that distinction.

Source: [Greek line 1.50](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.50)

## Σμινθεῦ (Smintheu) → आखुपते

**Status:** traditional semantic interpretation of a Greek cult epithet; NOT an established Greek–Sanskrit cognate  
**Lines:** 1.39

**Preferred Sanskrit:** आखुपतिः, vocative आखुपते. Homer addresses Apollo with Σμινθεῦ (*Smintheu*), the vocative of *Smintheus*. Ancient interpretations associate the epithet with σμίνθος (*sminthos*), “mouse,” although the historical derivation is disputed. “Lord of mice” is therefore an interpretive rendering, not a proved etymology. The name *Smintheu* remains in the Greek and its Roman transliteration.

**Vedic comparison 1, TS 1.8.6 (Rudra):** **आखुस्ते रुद्र पशुस्तं जुषस्व।** (“The mouse is your animal, O Rudra; accept it.”) This is an explicit ritual association, not evidence of name cognacy or identity between Apollo and Rudra. [TS 1.8.6, text and traditional notes](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/saMhitA/sarva-prastutiH/1/8_rAjasUyAdi/06_chAturmAsyeShu_sAkamedhaparva-tryambaka-haviH/).

**Vedic comparison 2, TB 1.1.3 (Agni):** **अग्निर्देवेभ्यो निलायत । आखू रूपं कृत्वा । स पृथिवीं प्राविशत् ।** Agni hides from the gods, assumes a mouse's form, and enters the earth. The ensuing explanation includes **आखुकरीष**. This is an independent Vedic ritual motif; it does not establish an Apollo–Agni equation. [Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 1.1.3, Sanskrit with commentary](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/brAhmaNam/sarva-prastutiH/1/1_agnyAdheyAdi/03_deva-yajanArtha-sambhArAH/).

**Greek source:** [Homer, Iliad 1.39 (Venetus A)](https://www.homermultitext.org/docs/venetus-a/12v/).

## ἀργυρέοιο βιοῖο (argureoio bioio) → रजतधनुषः

**Status:** Vedic poetic parallel, not a lexical cognate
**Lines:** 1.49
**Forms:** रजतधनुषो, रजतधनुषः

Homer describes Apollo's silver bow (ἀργυρέοιο βιοῖο). The Atharvaveda describes Rudra bearing a golden bow:

धनुर्बिभर्षि हरितं हिरण्ययं सहस्रघ्नि शतवधं शिखण्डिन् ।
रुद्रस्येषुश्चरति देवहेतिस्तस्यै नमो यतमस्यां दिशीतः ॥

Atharvaveda (Śaunaka) 11.2.12.

Both Apollo and Rudra are formidable divine archers. The comparison is poetic and theological, not an established linguistic cognate. The Sanskrit translation retains रजतधनुः because Homer's bow is silver.

**Sources**: [Homer, Iliad 1.49](https://homer.johnhboyer.com/iliad/book/1/?loc=1.49) · [Atharvaveda (Śaunaka) 11.2](https://www.vedicsamhita.in/vedas/atharvaveda/11/2)

## Διός (Dios) → दिवः

**Status:** established cognate
**Lines:** 1.63, 1.86
**Forms:** दिवः

Greek *Diós* and Vedic *Diváḥ* are historically related sky-god name forms. These occurrences refer to Zeus specifically. [Greek source](https://primarytexts.org/homer/iliad/1/).

## νήεσσι (nēessi) → नौकानाम्

**Status:** established cognate
**Lines:** 1.71, 1.89
**Forms:** नौः, नौकानाम्

Greek *naus* and Sanskrit *nau-* descend from the same inherited boat term. The Sanskrit prose form नौकानाम् is a derivative, not an identical Greek inflection. [Greek source](https://primarytexts.org/homer/iliad/1/).

## θύγατρα (thugatra) → दुहितरम्

**Status:** established cognate
**Lines:** 1.95
**Forms:** दुहितरं, दुहितरम्

Greek *thugatēr* and Sanskrit *duhitṛ* are established inherited kinship cognates. [Greek source](https://primarytexts.org/homer/iliad/1/).

## ἑκηβόλος (hekēbolos) → दूरेहेतिः

**Status:** Vedic poetic parallel, not a lexical cognate
**Lines:** 1.96
**Forms:** दूरेहेतिः, दूरेहेतेः

Our agreed Sanskrit rendering for Apollo's far-shooting epithet is **दूरेहेतिः**. Compare TS 3.4.7.2 and Sāyaṇa's explanation: दूरेऽप्यवस्थितस्य प्रहारसाधनं हेतिर्यस्यासौ दूरेहेतिः। The original TS referent is not Apollo. [Sāyaṇa text](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/saMhitA/sAyaNaH/3/4/07/).

## μῆνιν (mēnin) → मन्युम्

**Status:** chosen Vedic semantic rendering; cognacy disputed
**Lines:** 1.75
**Forms:** मन्युं, मन्युम्

Greek μῆνις is translated as Vedic मन्यु in the running prose. Vedic मेनि belongs only in a cautious philological footnote; its relationship to Greek μῆνις is unproven. [Greek source](https://primarytexts.org/homer/iliad/1/).

## Χρύσης (Khrusēs) → क्रूसेसः

**Status:** Narrative and ritual comparison; not a lexical cognate
**Lines:** 1.11, 1.22, 1.33, 1.35, 1.42, 1.43, 1.50, 1.92, 1.100
**Forms:** क्रूसेसः, क्रूसेसं, क्रूसेसस्य

Chryses' insult by Agamemnon, invocation of Apollo, punishment of the army, and subsequent expiation recall the story of Vaka Dālbhi and Dhṛtarāṣṭra Vaicitravīrya in the Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā 10.6. Vaka offers black rice to Agni Rudravat, and a restorative Agni Surabhimat offering follows. A later Mahābhārata account (Śalyaparvan 9.42 in one numbering) presents an expanded narrative with a different ritual medium. Neither source proves that Apollo and Rudra are identical, or that either tradition directly borrowed from the other.
