# Greek–Sanskrit comparative lexicon

Statuses are deliberately conservative.

## μῆνις (mēnis) → मन्यु

**Status:** semantic; possible etymologies disputed  
**Lines:** 1.1

Manyu is our chosen literary translation. Vedic मेनि is a comparative lead only, not an established cognate. Consult the actual Vedic occurrence and an etymological dictionary before asserting a connection.

## Διός (Dios) → दिवः

**Status:** established cognate  
**Lines:** 1.5, 1.9, 1.21

The Zeus/Dyaus name belongs to an inherited Indo-European sky-god word family. Keep Zeus as the character; do not reduce him to an anonymous sky.

## θύγατρα (thugatra) → दुहितरम्

**Status:** established cognate  
**Lines:** 1.13

Greek thugatēr and Sanskrit duhitṛ are inherited kinship terms; the Greek accusative is matched with Sanskrit accusative.

## νῆας (nēas) → नौः

**Status:** established cognate  
**Lines:** 1.12

Greek naus and Sanskrit nau- belong to the same inherited ship/boat word family.

## θεοί (theoi) → देवाः

**Status:** semantic equivalent only  
**Lines:** 1.18

Do not claim that Greek theos and Sanskrit deva are established cognates.

## Ἀπόλλων (Apollōn) → अपोल्लोनः

**Status:** Greek name retained  
**Lines:** 1.14, 1.21

No secure Sanskrit divine-name cognate is being asserted; similarity of function is not etymological identity.

## ἑκηβόλος (hekēbolos) → दूरेहेतिः

**Status:** Vedic semantic/poetic parallel, **not established lexical cognate**  
**Lines:** Iliad 1.14, 1.21

Preferred Sanskrit **दूरेहेतिः** (“one whose weapon reaches from afar”), attested TS **3.4.7.2** in a passage describing **Mṛtyu as a Gandharva**. Sāyaṇa glosses:

> दूरेऽप्यवस्थितस्य प्रहारसाधनं हेतिर्यस्यासौ दूरेहेतिः।

“He whose weapon is a means of striking someone even far away.” Sanskrit inflections in our passage: **दूरेहेतेः** (genitive) and **दूरेहेतिम्** (accusative).

**Compare**, without substituting in the running translation: **दूरेवधः**, TS **4.5.8**, Śrī Rudram: **नमो अग्रेवधाय च दूरेवधाय च**. This parallel highlights striking from afar, while **दूरेहेतिः** highlights the long reach of the missile itself. Neither expression is proven cognate to Greek ἑκηβόλος, nor does the comparison identify Apollo with a Vedic deity.

Source: https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/taittirIyam/sArasvata-vibhAgaH/saMhitA/sAyaNaH/3/4/07/

## ἄποινα (apoina) → निष्क्रयधनम्

**Status:** semantic equivalent only  
**Lines:** 1.13, 1.20

Ransom or compensation offered to free Chryses’ daughter; not a cognate claim.
## γέρων (gerōn) → जरन् / जरन्त्

**Status:** established Indo-European cognate (Greek γέρων ~ Vedic járant-)  
**Lines:** 1.26, 1.33, 1.35

Greek γέρων, stem γεροντ-, 'aged man', is related to Vedic जरन्त् (*járant-*, 'old, aged') via the inherited aging root, including comparable participial morphology. In the running prose **वृद्ध** or **जरन्** may be used according to idiom. Evidence: IE-CoR Cognate Set 2825 (https://iecor.clld.org/cognatesets/2825), citing Beekes and Mayrhofer.

## πατρίς (patris) → पितृ / पितृभूमिः

**Status:** established cognate of the underlying kinship base, not necessarily a word-for-word cognate of the whole derivative  
**Lines:** 1.30

Greek πατρίς 'fatherland' is built on the Greek word for father, πατήρ, whose Sanskrit cognate is पितृ. Sanskrit **पितृभूमिः** faithfully brings out the inherited root but is a Sanskrit formation, not an asserted one-to-one cognate of the Greek derived word.

## αἰγῶν (aigōn) → अज / अजानाम्

**Status:** proposed/contested historical relationship; semantic equivalent in the running translation  
**Lines:** 1.41

Greek αἴξ (gen. αἰγός) and Sanskrit अज are both 'goat'; their historical relationship is debated in modern reconstructions, particularly the differences between Greek *aig-* and Indo-Iranian *ag-*. We use **अजानाम्** for fidelity while marking the etymology as uncertain.
